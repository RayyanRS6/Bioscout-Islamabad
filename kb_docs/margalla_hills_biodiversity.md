# Margalla Hills National Park Biodiversity

The Margalla Hills National Park, located in the foothills of the Himalayas, is a biodiversity hotspot in Islamabad. The park covers an area of 17,386 hectares and is home to diverse flora and fauna.

## Flora
The park features a mix of subtropical pine forests and scrub vegetation. Common tree species include:
- Chir Pine (Pinus roxburghii)
- Olive (Olea europaea)
- Wild Olive (Olea cuspidata)
- Acacia species
- Fig trees (Ficus species)

## Fauna
The park hosts a variety of wildlife:

### Mammals
- Common Leopard (Panthera pardus) - Rare but present
- Golden Jackal (Canis aureus)
- Rhesus Macaque (Macaca mulatta)
- Wild Boar (Sus scrofa)
- Jungle Cat (Felis chaus)

### Birds
- Black Kite (Milvus migrans)
- Cinereous Vulture (Aegypius monachus)
- Rose-ringed Parakeet (Psittacula krameri)
- House Crow (Corvus splendens)
- Various migratory birds during winter

### Reptiles
- Bengal Monitor Lizard (Varanus bengalensis)
- Various snake species

## Conservation Status
The park faces challenges from:
- Urban encroachment
- Illegal hunting
- Habitat fragmentation
- Invasive species like Lantana camara

## Best Observation Spots
1. Trail 3 - Good for bird watching
2. Trail 5 - Known for leopard sightings
3. Daman-e-Koh - Panoramic views and wildlife
4. Saidpur Village area - Rich in bird diversity
